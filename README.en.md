# Pipeline for self-taught and teaching material

A personal AI pipeline to **research, write, and produce** two kinds of material: full reports for learning topics on your own, and didactic or student books. It is not a SaaS nor an app: it is a workflow built on top of an AI agent (open-format `SKILL.md` skills), configured with your own rules and traceable to the source.

## What it is and what it is not

**It is a high-quality draft generator, not an unattended final product.**

This is not marketing skepticism: it was measured. The pipeline was run from scratch on the same outline of a conference talk written by hand, in a semi-blind run (see [the full comparison](ejemplos/informe-conferencia/comparacion-pipeline-vs-manual.md)). Result: it tied on didactics and data handling (9/10 on both), and lost on oratory (6/10) — it does not generate the speaker's own anecdote, nor register variation, nor the moment when the audience says the anchor phrase. Those things come from the author.

The honest deal: the pipeline hands you a draft **traceable to the source** (every fact with its citation and its solidity level), and you inject what only you have. In a 15-chapter didactic book, that means receiving the structured, verified manuscript with its pedagogical apparatus, and working on top of it — not starting from a blank page.

## How it works: four layers

| Layer | What it does | Tools |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Research** | Gathers data with provenance; every claim gets tagged (I-xx) with a solidity level: SOLID / VERIFIED VIA THIRD PARTIES / PROVISIONAL; disputed evidence is marked as disputed | research agent (deep research) + search over academic APIs (OpenAlex, arXiv, Semantic Scholar, Crossref...) + optional Elicit pass for structured paper extraction |
| **2. Write** | Writes from the single source with fixed rules: didactic structure (*what it is → why it matters → how it is used* + real example), figures in the body, one idea per paragraph, didactic apparatus per chapter (objectives, questions, key points, glossary) | own didactic-writing skill + anti-"AI tells" prose scan (crutch negation, triads, empty signposting...) |
| **3. Design** | Applies a coherent palette and typography; textbooks keep editorial typography (serif 11pt, 120–145% leading) | themes skill (color+typography) + design foundations |
| **4. Produce** | Final render (docx/PDF) and page-by-page visual verification against acceptance criteria | documents skill + automated visual review |

Cross-cutting rules: **single source** (nothing is claimed that is not in the research), **verdict = the lowest dimension** (a document scoring 9 on content and 4 on sources does not pass), and **editing in four passes** (draft → line editing → copyedit → blind read).

**Opening parameter `modo`** — when starting a topic you declare one of two routes for layer 1: `web` (default: open research on general sources) or `academic` (search restricted to Google Scholar, arXiv, SciELO, Redalyc, Semantic Scholar and open-access journals; the Elicit pass goes from optional to mandatory, and the bibliography lives from day 1 in a BibTeX `references.bib` rendered as an APA References section). Same pipeline, two source regimes.

## Examples

- **[Book: Software Development Stack with AI](ejemplos/libro-stack-con-ia/)** — a 15-chapter didactic book generated with the full pipeline: scoping interview, research with sources, three editorial passes per chapter, a final "acquiring editor" review reading the PDF blind. Full PDF included.
- **[Test against hand-made material](ejemplos/informe-conferencia/comparacion-pipeline-vs-manual.md)** — the same conference talk, written by hand vs. generated by the pipeline, compared fact by fact: 71% coverage, conflicts adjudicated against primary sources, and the per-dimension verdict behind this repo's positioning.
- **[Tutorial: customize your GitHub profile README](https://github.com/Jesus-Valverde/github-profile-readme-tutorial)** — a bilingual guide produced end-to-end with the pipeline in a single day: deep research (14 items, 33 verified facts), didactic drafting with four editing passes per version, and rendered deliverables (markdown, docx, PDF). Live on the author's profile.

## The research: what I evaluated, what I kept, what I rejected

About 20 tools and collections were evaluated during the build. The full record lives in [investigaciones.md](investigaciones.md). The moral across all of them: almost everything sold as an "AI generator of X" is an interface over the same models your own agent already orchestrates — the filter question that kills 90% of purchases is **"which phase of my pipeline does this solve, and what do I already have installed for that phase?"**

## How to replicate it

Step-by-step guide with commands: **[INSTALACION.md](INSTALACION.md)**. Summary:

The pipeline lives entirely in open-format skills (`SKILL.md` — the standard read by Claude Code, zCode, Cursor, Codex and friends):

1. **Get an agent that reads skills** and your own skills folder.
2. **Each layer is a skill**: research with solidity ratings, writing with your personal canon (this is the non-distributable part: YOUR rules), design themes, render. The third-party components come from open repos: [scientific-agent-skills](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills) (K-Dense, MIT), [bookwright](https://github.com/adrianmastronardi/bookwright) (book publishing pipeline, MIT), [anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills) (themes and documents, Apache-2.0), plus own adaptations from the [Xamfonos technical writing guide](https://github.com/Xamfonos/technical-writing-best-practices) and Book Genesis's [AI-tells](https://github.com/felipelobomotta-blip/book-genesis-studio) (MIT).
3. **Structure your project** per deliverable: topic folder → `investigacion.md` (single source) → draft → render.
4. **Version everything** — a book is weeks of work; without git it is a gamble.
